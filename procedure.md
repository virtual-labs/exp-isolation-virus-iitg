### Procedure

#### Sample preparation
Field samples from a virus outbreak, such as cloacal swabs, are collected and diluted accordingly in Phosphate-Buffered Saline (PBS). Additionally, a cocktail of antibiotics, including Penicillin, Streptomycin, and Gentamicin, as well as antifungals such as Amphotericin B, is added to remove any unwanted bacterial and fungal contaminants in the samples.

#### Virus inoculation
Let us assume the virus present in the sample is Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV). To further propagate the virus, two systems will be adapted:
1. **Specific Pathogen Free (SPF) Embryonated Chicken Eggs:** NDV has a high affinity for the chorioallantoic membrane (CAM) and the cells lining the allantoic cavity. Inoculation in 9-11 day old SPF eggs will allow the virus to replicate in high titers.
2. **Cell culture system (In vitro):** The virus will be passaged in susceptible cell lines such as DF-1 or primary chicken embryonic fibroblasts (CEF). Cytopathic effects (CPE) are typically visible after 24-48 hours of virus infection, providing visual confirmation of the virus.

#### Molecular characterization and sequencing
After the successful isolation and amplification of the virus, its molecular characterization must be performed. Since NDV is a negative-sense RNA virus, the viral genome must be extracted from the allantoic fluid and cell culture supernatant using Trizol. Complementary DNA (cDNA) has to be synthesized using the enzyme Reverse transcriptase, as sequencing of the viral genome cannot be performed using RNA as a template. By sequencing key genes such as the Fusion (F) gene of NDV, the isolated virus can be categorized as lentogenic (mild), mesogenic (moderate), or velogenic (highly virulent).
